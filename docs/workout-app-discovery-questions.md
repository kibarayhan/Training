# Discovery Grilling: Questions You Should Answer Before Building

Companion to `workout-app-market-research.md`. Each section lists the decisions
hiding inside your idea, why they matter, and — where I have a strong opinion —
a recommendation. Answer these before writing code; every "we'll figure it out
later" here becomes a rewrite later.

---

## 0. The ambiguity in your own request

**"I want to use AI to build this app" — which one do you mean?**
1. AI as a *development tool* (Claude Code / Cursor writing the Swift code)?
2. AI as a *product feature* (AI-generated plans, adaptive coaching, natural-language workout creation)?
3. Both?

Why it matters: (1) affects tech-stack choice and your role (reviewer vs
programmer). (2) is a product positioning decision that changes scope by months
and adds ongoing inference costs. Don't conflate them.

---

## 1. Who is it for? (the question that decides everything else)

- Are you building this **for yourself first** and productizing later, or for a market from day one? (Be honest — it changes how much validation you need.)
- Runner-first, cyclist-first, or truly both at launch? Their needs diverge more than you think: cyclists live on **power**, runners on **pace**; cyclists need FTP tests and smart-trainer thinking, runners need GAP (grade-adjusted pace) and race-distance predictors.
- What about **triathletes**? They're the highest-paying segment (TrainingPeaks' core) but demand swim support, brick workouts, and multi-sport days.
- **Self-coached athletes only**, or do you want a coach→athlete relationship (coach builds plan, athlete executes)? Coach features are a second product; TrainingPeaks' moat is coaches, not athletes.
- Beginner ("couch to 5K") or experienced ("knows what TSS means")? The UI, vocabulary, and onboarding are completely different products. You cannot serve both well at launch.
- Apple-Watch-only athletes, or people who own Garmin/Wahoo head units too? If the latter, they'll ask "why can't I send workouts to my Garmin?" in week one.

## 2. Workout builder — the details you haven't specified

- **Step types**: warm-up, cool-down, work, recovery, rest — do you also need *repeat blocks* (e.g., 6× [3min @ threshold / 2min easy])? Nested repeats? (Apple WorkoutKit supports one level of interval blocks — deeper nesting won't sync.)
- **Step duration basis**: time only, or also distance ("800m repeats"), calorie, and **"lap-button press"** (open-ended step the athlete ends manually — essential for real interval training)?
- **Intensity target types**: power (watts, %FTP, W/kg?), heart rate (bpm, %LTHR, %max, zones?), pace (min/km, %threshold pace, GAP?), cadence, RPE (no sensor at all), or *none* (just "easy")? Which are must-have at launch?
- **Ranges vs single targets**: "230–250W" vs "240W ±5%"? How do you display/edit a range quickly on a phone?
- **Primary + secondary targets**: "hold 250W AND keep cadence above 90" — supported or not? (WorkoutKit: one alert per step.)
- **Ramps**: steps where the target slides from 100W to 250W over 10 min (warm-ups)? Garmin/Zwift support these; WorkoutKit doesn't. Do you approximate with mini-steps or drop the feature?
- **Workout library**: do users only build from scratch, or do you ship a **template library** (classic sessions: 2×20 threshold, 30/30s, tempo runs)? Who authors that content — you? Licensed from a coach? Generated by AI?
- **Duplicating/editing scheduled workouts**: if a user edits a workout that's already on next week's calendar, does the calendar copy change too? (Decide: workouts as reusable templates vs one-off instances. Get this data model right on day one.)
- **Import/export**: `.fit`, `.zwo` (Zwift), `.erg/.mrc`, TrainingPeaks format? Import is a huge adoption lever (people bring their existing library); export prevents lock-in complaints.
- Can a user **share** a workout with a friend (link/QR)? Cheap viral loop — in or out?

## 3. Zones, thresholds, and athlete profile

- Where do FTP / threshold HR / threshold pace come from — manual entry, guided test protocols (20-min test, ramp test), auto-detection from completed rides, or import from Intervals.icu/Strava?
- Do thresholds have **history**? (Your fitness math breaks silently if a January workout is scored against a July FTP.)
- Separate zones per sport? (Running HR zones ≠ cycling HR zones — yes, they must be separate.)
- How many zone models do you support — Coggan classic 7-zone, 5-zone, custom? Or do you hide zones entirely and just use % of threshold?
- What happens for a **brand-new user with no data**? Day-one experience with empty state is where most fitness apps lose people. Estimate from age/weight? Force a test week?

## 4. Planning & periodization

- Is a "plan" just workouts placed on a calendar, or a first-class object — a named multi-week block with a goal ("Build to marathon on Oct 12") that can be shifted, scaled, or regenerated as a unit?
- **Recurring scheduling**: "every Tuesday is intervals" — supported? What happens on conflicts?
- **Life happens**: user misses Tuesday's workout — does the plan auto-reshuffle (that's adaptive planning, a big feature), prompt the user, or just show it as missed? This single decision separates a calendar app from a coaching app.
- Do you support **target events/races** (a date the plan builds toward, with taper)? Your fitness *projection* feature is 10× more compelling framed as "form on race day."
- **Rest weeks / periodization structure** (3 weeks on, 1 week off)? Built into plan templates or left to the user?
- Weekly **load targets** ("this week: 400 TSS")? Do you warn when a user schedules a 40% load jump (injury risk)?
- How far ahead can users plan — is 6 months a supported case? (Affects your 15-workout Apple sync window logic and projection UI.)

## 5. Apple integration — edge cases that will eat your support inbox

- The user does Tuesday's workout **on Wednesday** — does it match? Match window rules?
- The user does a *different* workout than scheduled (rode free instead of intervals) — is that "completed," "substituted," or "missed"? Do you compare planned vs actual step-by-step (compliance score)?
- Two activities on one day, or a scheduled workout done twice — which matches?
- Activity recorded **outside Apple** (Garmin watch, Zwift) lands in Apple Health via sync — do you match those too? Duplicate detection when the same ride arrives from both Strava and HealthKit?
- User declines HealthKit permissions — is the app useless or degraded-but-usable?
- **Watch-less users**: can an iPhone-only runner still use the app (audio cues while running with phone)? Or is Apple Watch required hardware? Say so on the App Store page.
- Do you build your **own Watch app** for guided workouts (full control, big effort) or rely purely on WorkoutKit sync into Apple's native Workout app (fast, but Apple owns the in-workout experience — no pre-step countdown, one alert per step)? **Recommendation: WorkoutKit-only for MVP; own Watch app only if traction.**
- watchOS/iOS minimum versions? (WorkoutKit realistically means iOS 17+ / watchOS 10+ — fine, but decide.)

## 6. Fitness model & projection — the math you're signing up for

- Which load metric when data is missing? Power ride → TSS; no power → hrTSS; no HR either → RPE × duration? You need a documented **fallback chain** or gaps corrupt the model.
- Combined fitness or per-sport? (Run fitness and bike fitness are not interchangeable; TrainingPeaks tracks one combined CTL, Intervals.icu can split. Which story do you tell?)
- Do you show raw **CTL/ATL/TSB** numbers (experts expect them) or translate to plain language ("Fitness 62, Fatigue 71, you're carrying fatigue")? Both?
- Projection assumes **100% plan compliance** — do you also model "at your typical 80% compliance you'll hit CTL 55, not 70"? (That's honest and differentiating.)
- Estimated TSS of a *planned* workout: computed from steps (doable) — but what about planned workouts with no structure ("2h easy ride")? Need duration × assumed IF defaults.
- Do you incorporate **recovery/wellness inputs** (sleep, HRV, resting HR — all in HealthKit) to adjust readiness, or is that scope creep for v1? (It's scope creep. But decide eyes-open, because Whoop/Garmin have trained users to expect it.)
- What's your answer when a sports scientist on Reddit says your model is wrong? (Serious question — publish your formulas like Intervals.icu, or keep them opaque like Xert?)

## 7. AI *in* the product (if you meant that)

- **Natural-language workout creation** ("give me a 1-hour threshold session, 3 intervals") → structured steps. High wow-factor, well-bounded, cheap. Probably the best first AI feature.
- **AI plan generation** ("I race a half marathon June 3, can train 5h/week") — this is where JOIN/Athletica/TrainerRoad compete. Do you want to fight there at launch? What's your defensible angle?
- **Adaptive adjustment** (missed workouts / poor performance reshapes the plan) — the hardest one; needs the fitness model + compliance data working first.
- Post-workout **AI summaries** ("your HR drifted 8% in the final interval...") — cheap, nice retention feature.
- Where does inference run — on-device (Apple Intelligence, free, private) vs API (Claude/GPT, better quality, per-user cost)? What's your inference budget per subscriber per month?
- Liability line: AI giving *training advice* to humans with heart-rate conditions — where's your disclaimer, and do you cap what advice it gives? (See §10.)

## 8. Integrations beyond Apple

- **Strava**: read activities? Post workouts? (Their API terms restrict using data for ML/training and require branding compliance — read them before building.) Realistically: users will demand "sync completed workouts *to* Strava."
- **Intervals.icu / TrainingPeaks export** — friendly bridge or competitor stance?
- **Garmin/Wahoo push** (structured workout to head unit) — v2 promise? Garmin's API requires a business application/approval; start the paperwork early if it's on the roadmap.
- **Zwift** — indoor cyclists will ask. `.zwo` export is the cheap answer.
- Smart trainer control (ERG mode over Bluetooth) — almost certainly **no** (that's building TrainerRoad), but decide and write it down.

## 9. Platform & tech stack

- **iOS-native (Swift/SwiftUI)** vs cross-platform (Flutter/React Native)? Your killer feature is WorkoutKit — a native-only API that cross-platform frameworks wrap poorly. **Recommendation: native Swift, iOS-only MVP; Android only if the business proves out.** (This also matters for AI-assisted development: Swift+SwiftUI is well within Claude/Cursor competence.)
- **Backend or no backend?** CloudKit gives you sync, backup, and zero server cost/ops — but locks data in Apple's world and makes a future Android/web version harder. Custom backend (Supabase/Firebase/your own) costs money and time now. **Recommendation: CloudKit or local-first for MVP; don't build a server before you have users.**
- Web companion app for plan building on a big screen? (TrainingPeaks/Intervals users live on desktop for planning. Painful to skip, expensive to build. Defer, but acknowledge.)
- Your own skill level: can you review/debug Swift that AI writes? If not, what's your plan when the AI gets HealthKit permission handling subtly wrong?

## 10. Legal, privacy, and App Store reality

- Workout + heart-rate data is **health data**: GDPR (you're presumably in the EU — kibarayhan@gmail — so this applies to *you* as data controller), plus Apple's rule that HealthKit data can't be used for advertising. Privacy policy required before App Store submission.
- Where is user data stored, and can users **export/delete everything**? (GDPR right-to-erasure; also a selling point.)
- Training advice disclaimer ("consult a physician") — standard, but decide whether AI-generated advice needs stronger guardrails (e.g., never advise training through chest pain, hard cap on load ramps).
- App Store review: health apps get extra scrutiny; HealthKit entitlement usage descriptions must be precise or you get rejected.

## 11. Money

- **Business intent**: personal tool that pays for itself, indie lifestyle business (~$100K/yr), or venture-scale ambition? (Research doc says the market only supports the first two — but *your* intent decides how much you invest before revenue.)
- Free tier line: what's free forever vs paid? (Recommendation from research doc: builder + Watch sync free; calendar/matching/projection paid.)
- Price point: $5.99/mo? $49/yr? Lifetime option? (Lifetime is cash now, regret later.) Regional pricing?
- Trial: 7-day, 14-day, or freemium-only?
- What's your **budget for the boring costs**: Apple dev account ($99/yr), AI coding subscriptions, App Store screenshots/design, TestFlight beta incentives, LLC/legal, and — if AI features — inference?
- **Kill criteria**: what number by what date means you stop? (Suggested from research: <500 paying subscribers after 12 months of real marketing = wind down or keep as personal tool.)

## 12. Go-to-market (the part indie devs always skip)

- What's the **one-sentence pitch** that isn't true of Intervals.icu or TrainingPeaks? ("Training plans that live natively on your Apple Watch" is the candidate from the research — do you agree?)
- Where do your first 100 users come from, concretely? (r/AppleWatch, r/running, r/Velo, TrainerRoad forum, a build-in-public Twitter/Mastodon, your own club?)
- Will you pursue reviewers (DC Rainmaker, GPLama, Shervin)? They make or break niche fitness apps — and they'll compare you to incumbents on day one. What must be true before you invite that comparison?
- Beta plan: TestFlight with how many athletes, for how long, through which season? (Fitness apps need a *training cycle* of beta time — weeks, not days.)
- App Store Optimization: what search terms do you want to own? ("structured workout apple watch", "cycling training plan"?)

## 13. Operational reality

- Who answers support email when HealthKit sync silently fails on someone's Watch Ultra? (It will.)
- Analytics/crash reporting (privacy-respecting: TelemetryDeck?) — decided up front or bolted on?
- How do you test Watch sync without owning every Watch model? (TestFlight beta cohort is your device lab — recruit accordingly.)
- Accessibility & localization: English-only at launch? (Fine — but the endurance market is very German/Dutch/French-heavy. Plan for strings from day one.)
- Offline behavior: workouts must sync to Watch *before* the user leaves WiFi. What's the failure UX at the trailhead?

## The four questions to answer first

1. **What did "use AI" mean** — dev tool, product feature, or both?

### Product-detail decisions (round 2, answered 2026-07-11)

| # | Question | Decision | Consequences |

| 1 | Intensity target types | **All four: power (bike), pace (run), HR (both), cadence/RPE** | Full target-editor surface; per-sport zone models (power zones, pace zones, separate run/bike HR zones) in the data model from day one. RPE steps mean "no sensor target" must be a valid step state. |
| 2 | Repeat blocks | **Yes, single-level** (6×[on/off]) | Data model: workout → items, where an item is a step or a repeat-block of steps. Maps 1:1 to both WorkoutKit interval blocks and FIT workout repeat steps — no flattening needed anywhere. No nesting. |

| 5 | Thresholds | **Manual entry + dated history** | `ThresholdRecord(sport, kind, value, validFrom)` — every activity/workout is scored against the threshold valid on its date. Auto-detection (eFTP-style) deferred to v2. |
| 6 | Plan matching | **±1 day auto-match, same sport, manual re-link override** | Compliance states: completed / substituted (matched but different structure) / missed / unplanned. Fitness projection consumes actual-when-matched, planned otherwise. |

### New requirement: export & third-party head units (added 2026-07-11)

- **MVP: FIT workout file export** — the lingua franca. A structured-workout `.fit` can be dropped onto a Garmin (`NewFiles/` via USB / Garmin Connect import) and imported into Hammerhead's dashboard for the Karoo. Single-level repeats, time/distance/open steps, and power/pace/HR/cadence targets all map cleanly.
- **Consider at MVP (cheap): `.zwo` export** for Zwift trainer sessions (power-based workouts only).
- **v2: direct platform sync** — Garmin Training API (push workouts to device automatically; requires Garmin developer-program approval — apply early, approval takes time) and Hammerhead/TrainingPeaks-style integrations. Also inbound: pull completed activities from Garmin/Strava so the fitness model sees rides that never touch HealthKit.
- Design rule this creates: **the internal workout model must be defined as the superset that exports losslessly to both WorkoutKit and FIT** — build the model against those two targets from the first line of code, not retrofitted.
